🧬 Investigational combination

EloraTZP

Eli Lilly's experimental pairing — Elora + TZP — designed to hit three hormone pathways at once. The Phase 2b results beat TZP alone on weight and blood sugar. Here's what the trial actually found, in plain language.

01

What it is

Two medicines, one strategy — not a single new molecule.

"Elora" + "TZP" — the name is the recipe. Elora plus TZP in one strategy. A combination approach, not one engineered triple-agonist.

EloraTZP is Eli Lilly's development name for pairing Elora — a selective amylin receptor agonist — with TZP, the dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist already used in Mounjaro and Zepbound.

The two halves sit at different stages: TZP is an established prescription medicine, while Elora is still investigational (it's also being studied on its own in Phase 3 obesity trials). Lilly designed the combination with minimal activity at the calcitonin receptor.

One thing to keep straight: approval of TZP does not make the combination approved, and it doesn't establish the combo's long-term safety.

02

Three pathways at once

Each pathway pulls a different lever on hunger and blood sugar.

🎯

Amylin — the fullness signal

Elora activates amylin receptors (mainly AMY1). Amylin is released after eating and signals satiety — the "I'm satisfied" feeling. This is the new lever the combo adds.

🍽️

GLP-1 — appetite + glucose

TZP's first pathway: steadies appetite, helps manage blood glucose, and slows stomach emptying — so less food goes further.

⚡

GIP — the incretin partner

TZP's second pathway: drives glucose-dependent insulin release and works together with GLP-1 signaling on metabolism.

🔀

Not the same trio as Reta

Reta is one molecule hitting GIP + GLP-1 + glucagon. EloraTZP is two medicines hitting GIP + GLP-1 + amylin. Three pathways each — but a different third one, so don't treat them as equivalent.

03

The Phase 2b trial

The study behind the headlines — who was in it and how it ran.

367
Adults enrolled

With obesity or overweight plus type 2 diabetes — a group where big, lasting weight loss has been hard to achieve.

48
Weeks

Double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (NCT06603571) run in the US and Argentina.

105.4 kg
Average starting weight

About 232 lbs, with an average HbA1c of 8.1%.

📅 Presented Sept 30, 2026

Results were announced by Lilly and presented at the European Association for the Study of Diabetes annual meeting in Milan. Four EloraTZP regimens were tested against Elora alone, TZP alone, and placebo.

In this trial the two medicines were given as two separate pen injections — the combination name didn't mean one premixed shot.

05

Blood sugar

Weight wasn't the only thing that moved.

−2.9
A1C points · top combo

Average HbA1c drop from a starting point of 8.1%.

−2.4
A1C points · TZP alone

Combo regimens ranged from 2.2 to 2.9 points of reduction.

A1C reflects average blood glucose over roughly two to three months — so this is a meaningful improvement in diabetes control, not just the scale moving.

Two caveats: these results don't prove effects on heart attacks, kidney disease, or other long-term complications — those need their own evidence. And the trial enrolled people with type 2 diabetes, so don't assume the same numbers for people without it.

06

Side effects & tolerability

The part of the story the headline skips.

🤢

Mostly gastrointestinal

Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, abdominal discomfort — generally described as mild to moderate, clustering during dose escalation. More frequent with the combo than with either medicine alone.

📊

Staying power matters

Discontinuation due to side effects ran ~11–27% across combo arms, vs 2.9% for TZP alone. Weight lost only counts if people can stay on the treatment.

More pathways can mean more benefit and more to tolerate — a combo isn't automatically better just because it targets more receptors. The real question is whether the extra benefit justifies the extra burden for each person.

Because EloraTZP is investigational, its full side-effect profile isn't established yet. Severe or persistent digestive symptoms warrant medical assessment, not toughing it out.

07

The bottom line

The whole page in one checklist.

Promising mid-stage data — not an approved medicine.

  • Investigational only. Not approved by any regulator; no established clinical use outside trials.
  • Phase 3 is planned, not done. Lilly aims to start trials of a co-formulated (single-product) version in Q4 2026 — a milestone, not a guarantee.
  • The trial used two separate injections. A future single formulation needs its own testing — don't assume it behaves identically.
  • Population-specific results. Adults with obesity/overweight and type 2 diabetes — not a promise for anyone else.
  • Online products aren't the trial drug. Anything sold under the name hasn't been shown to match the trial formulation's identity, purity, or safety.

🔬

Different third lever

Reta’s glucagon activity is being investigated for its role in energy expenditure; Elora’s amylin activity targets satiety and food intake. Same count of pathways, different biology.

⚠️

Don’t crown a winner yet

24.2% vs 23.3% comes from separate trials — different people, durations, and designs. A real head-to-head would need its own trial.
